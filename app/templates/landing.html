The problem

Student comments are collected, but rarely heard in time.

{{ icon('history', 20) }}

Reviewed too late

Feedback is usually read at the end of the term, after the chance to fix things has passed.

{{ icon('users', 20) }}

Read inconsistently

Reading comments by hand is slow, and different readers judge the same comment differently.

{{ icon('layers', 20) }}

Patterns stay hidden

Without grouping by week, course and topic, recurring concerns grow into larger complaints.

How it works

A simple weekly feedback loop

  1. 1

    Collect

    A feedback window opens each week. Students send short, anonymous comments about their courses.

  2. 2

    Analyze

    Text and sentiment analysis labels every comment positive, negative or neutral, and groups it by topic.

  3. 3

    Summarize

    Results are combined by week, course, department and college into dashboards and a weekly report.

  4. 4

    Act

    Alerts flag rising concerns. Staff record what they did, and students see the response.

Features

Everything needed to turn feedback into action

{{ icon('pencil', 20) }}

Anonymous weekly feedback

One comment per course each week. No names are stored, and duplicate submissions are blocked without tracking who wrote what.

{{ icon('smile', 20) }}

Automatic sentiment analysis

A lightweight text-analysis model reads English, Filipino and Taglish, handling negation like "hindi maganda" and emphasis like "sobrang init".

{{ icon('tag', 20) }}

Topics and keywords

Comments are grouped into concerns such as instruction, facilities, workload, enrollment and safety, with recurring keywords highlighted.

{{ icon('trend', 20) }}

Trend dashboards

See sentiment week by week for the whole university, a college, a department or a single course.

{{ icon('bell', 20) }}

Alerts and follow-up

Sharp rises in negative comments, recurring topics and safety concerns are flagged, and every response is tracked.

{{ icon('reports', 20) }}

Weekly reports

A print-ready summary is generated every week, with CSV export for further analysis.

Privacy

Built around student anonymity

PSU Pulse is designed in line with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173). Students can speak freely because nothing they write is linked back to them.
